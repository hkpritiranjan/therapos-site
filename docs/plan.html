§1 — Product Vision

TherapOS is the AI-native operating system for independent therapists. It is not an EHR. It is not a therapy app. It is not another AI chatbot companion. It is the AI copilot that therapists open before every session — and cannot practice without after 30 days of use.

The product handles the clinical workflow that happens around therapy sessions: preparation, documentation, memory, and search. The AI remembers everything, prepares everything, and handles the busywork. The therapist remains completely in control.

The 10-year vision: Every session makes the AI smarter about that patient. After 20 sessions, TherapOS understands the treatment arc better than any EHR ever could. Eventually, the question is not "can a therapist use TherapOS?" — it is "can a therapist practice as well without it?" The answer becomes no. That is the product goal: not lock-in through friction, but lock-in through indispensability.

§2 — Positioning

Category: AI Practice Management — not EHR, not therapy app, not AI chatbot companion.

One-liner: "AI clinical notes and patient memory for therapists — without recording your sessions."

Previous one-liner (retired): "The AI copilot independent therapists open before every session — and can't practice without." Retired because the claim of indispensability is a hope, not a position.

Tag line alternatives:

  • "Your notes should already know the patient."
  • "Document less. Prepare more. Record nothing."
  • "AI that reads your notes, not your sessions."

Versus: EHR-bundled AI (SimplePractice Note Taker, TherapyNotes TherapyFuel) — locked to one EHR. Ambient AI scribes (Upheal, Mentalyc, Freed, Heidi) — record the session and carry consent and retention obligations. Enterprise tools (Eleos) — sold to organisations. TherapOS is the EHR-agnostic, text-first option for individual clinicians.

Target ICP priority:

  1. Independent US therapists (LCSW, MFT, PhD, LPC, LMHC) in private practice — highest pain, highest willingness to pay, fastest sales cycle, most referral-network-dense
  2. Small US clinics (2–10 therapists) where the practice owner is also a clinician
  3. Independent therapists in India — large market, lower WTP, separate pricing, DPDP Act compliance required

§4 — Why Therapists Would Switch

They don't have to switch anything. TherapOS explicitly excludes billing, scheduling, and insurance. The pitch: "Keep SimplePractice for billing. Use TherapOS for everything clinical." Zero migration cost, zero switching risk.

The value is immediate: a therapist can generate their first AI note within 10 minutes of signup, without importing any data. The compound value (memory, patterns, search) builds over months and creates the natural switching cost that keeps them.

Contrast with SimplePractice: switching SimplePractice requires migrating billing records, insurance history, scheduling, and patient intake forms — a multi-day project. TherapOS's add-on positioning eliminates this barrier entirely.

§5 — The Moat

Compound memory (hypothesis): Every finalized note deepens the patient summary. Competitors also advertise cross-session features, so memory alone is not a moat. It becomes one only if ours is meaningfully better and inspectable: every summary sentence linked to its source note, editable by the therapist, and exportable so the therapist trusts it rather than feels trapped by it.

Workflow lock-in: Patient histories, notes, goals, and patterns are in TherapOS. The switching cost is not software — it's two years of clinical context. Therapists feel this acutely.

Aggregate intelligence (V2 → V3, speculative): Using de-identified patterns across clients to improve the AI requires explicit opt-in, a defensible de-identification method, and likely legal review under HIPAA and state law. Treat it as an option, not an assumption.

Speed advantage (withdrawn): An earlier version claimed a 24-month structural head start over incumbents. Upheal and Mentalyc already ship cross-session features and SimplePractice has an AI note taker, so there is no evidence for that figure. The real advantages to build are trust (no recording, transparent data flow), EHR-agnosticism, and price.

§7 — MVP Scope

The MVP solves exactly one painful problem with extreme quality: AI-generated, context-aware session notes that actually know the patient. Everything else can wait.

MVP ships with: Therapist signup + Clerk auth, patient profiles (name/concerns/modality/diagnoses), session creation, raw notes input, AI SOAP/DAP note generation (streaming via SSE), editable notes editor, one-click finalization, session prep brief (from session 2 onward), treatment goals (CRUD + progress notes), patient session timeline, dashboard with today's sessions.

Not in MVP: natural language search (add in Q4 2026), homework generator, multi-therapist accounts, India launch, mobile app, billing integration, public API.

✓
MVP success signal

A therapist generates their first AI note within 10 minutes of signup. They return for session 2 without any prompting. At 30 days they say, unprompted, "I can't imagine going back."

§15 — Memory Architecture

The memory system is the structural moat. Three tiers, each serving a different purpose:

Tier 1 — Redis

Active session state. Session prep brief cache (24h TTL). In-progress note draft. Fast reads during live sessions. No persistence beyond TTL.

Tier 2 — PostgreSQL

Rolling AI patient summary (3–5 sentences). Updated after every finalized note via BullMQ job. Injected into every AI call for this patient.

Tier 3 — pgvector

Every finalized note embedded (text-embedding-3-small, 1536d). Searched via cosine similarity for NL search. Permanent — enables recall of any session content.

§16 — Vector Search Design

pgvector with HNSW indexing was chosen over Pinecone for the MVP. Reasons: no extra infrastructure, cost efficiency at scale below 500k embeddings, and PostgreSQL's row-level security means therapist data isolation is enforced at the index level without additional middleware.

Migration path: if vector count exceeds 500k or metadata filtering complexity outgrows pgvector, migrate to Pinecone. This is a single-service change — the rest of the system doesn't need to change.

§17 — Auth Flow

Authentication via Clerk. No custom auth — Clerk handles session management, MFA, OAuth (Google, Apple), and device trust. Clerk webhooks sync user creation/deletion events to the TherapOS User table. The ClerkGuard NestJS guard verifies the JWT on every API call and extracts the therapist ID for row-level isolation.

§18–20 — User Journeys

New Therapist Onboarding

Sign up → set license + modality → dashboard (empty state: "Add your first patient") → create patient → create session → run session → generate AI note → finalize → aha moment. Target: first note in under 10 minutes.

Returning Therapist (weekly use)

Open TherapOS → dashboard shows today's sessions → click patient → read 90-second prep brief → run session → type raw notes → generate AI draft → review and edit → finalize → move to next patient. Total time: 3–5 minutes per session.

Patient Profile Journey

Open patient → Overview (summary, active goals, stats) → Timeline (full history) → search "when did we first talk about X?" → find session → open note → reference for today's session. TherapOS is the memory layer between sessions — not just the documentation tool.

§30 — Pricing Strategy

See the Pricing page for full detail.

The logic: $29/month undercuts EHR AI add-ons (SimplePractice Note Taker ~$35, TherapyFuel ~$40, both requiring a base EHR plan) and most standalone scribes (Upheal notes ~$39, Freed from $39). Only Mentalyc's entry tier (~$19.99, 40 notes) is lower. At $150/session, $29 is under a fifth of one session. Caveat: a $29 seat has a low revenue ceiling (Research §2), so the plan adds an earlier clinic tier and tests $19 / $29 / $39 on the waitlist before fixing price.

§31 — Go-To-Market Strategy

Phase 1 — Unscalable (months 1–3): Find 10 therapists personally. Offer 6 months free for a commitment to weekly 30-minute product feedback calls. Watch them use it over Zoom screen share. Fix every friction point within 24 hours. Create 10 raving fans before acquiring 100 users.

Phase 2 — Community (months 2–6): Independent therapist communities are dense and referral-heavy. Target:

  • Facebook Groups: "Private Practice Therapists" and "Therapists in Business" (member counts previously cited here were unverified and have been removed; confirm current size and each group's self-promotion rules before posting)
  • Reddit: r/therapists, r/socialwork, r/psychotherapy
  • LinkedIn: therapist networks, NASW groups
  • Therapist podcasts: 20+ shows with 5k–50k listeners each

Phase 3 — SEO (months 3–12): Content for high-intent search terms:

  • "SOAP note template therapist"
  • "How to write DAP notes faster"
  • "AI therapy notes HIPAA" (only publish once our own compliance claims are true)
  • "Session prep template therapist"
  • Monthly search volumes previously listed here had no source. Pull current numbers from a keyword tool before prioritising.

Phase 4 — Referral program: "Give 1 month, get 1 month." Therapists are deeply trusted by their colleagues. Peer referral is commonly the strongest channel in professional communities, but the "5–8x" multiple we cited earlier was unsourced; measure our own conversion by channel.

§32–34 — First 100 Customers

Week 1–2: Contact 50 therapists in your personal network. Target: 10 beta users. Offer 6 months free.

Week 3–4: Post in 3 therapist Facebook groups. Share genuine context: "I'm building an AI tool for therapists and need 10 people to test it." Don't sell. Invite.

Month 2: Each beta user refers 2 people. You're at 30 users. Personal onboarding call for every new user — understand their workflow before you try to change it.

Month 3: Product Hunt launch (Tuesday, 12:01am PST) + Show HN simultaneously. Beta users share in their networks. Target: 500 signups, 50 conversions.

Month 4–6: Start converting betas from free to paid. Your 10 original users are now advocates. Ask each for a testimonial and a video walkthrough. Use testimonials in all acquisition channels.

Month 6 target: 100 paying customers, $2,900 MRR, 60%+ month-4 retention, NPS >55.

§38 — Regulatory

HIPAA

Status: not yet compliant. The list below is the target control set, not a description of what exists today. See Case Study §7 for what is built.

  • Encrypt at rest (managed database encryption + application-layer envelope encryption for PHI fields) — planned
  • Encrypt in transit (TLS enforced, HSTS, no HTTP fallback) — partly in place via hosting
  • Audit logs on all PHI access, retained 6 years — table exists; logging not implemented
  • MFA for all accounts — planned
  • BAAs with every processor that handles PHI (cloud host, AI vendors, email, payments) — none executed. OpenAI and Anthropic BAAs are conditional on zero-retention-eligible endpoints and approval; see Research §6.
  • Annual security risk assessment; continuous-monitoring tool once there are paying customers — planned

Psychotherapy notes vs progress notes

HIPAA gives "psychotherapy notes" (45 CFR 164.501) extra protection when kept separate from the record. TherapOS finalized notes are progress notes in the record. Raw session notes the therapist types are the closest analogue to process notes, so they are treated as the most sensitive data: excluded from analytics, optionally kept off the AI path, and never shared across clinic accounts. Counsel must confirm this design.

State AI laws

Illinois (HB 1806, 2025) restricts AI from independent therapeutic decisions, unreviewed treatment plans and emotion or mental-state detection, while allowing administrative support. Nevada (AB 406) and others restrict AI-delivered care; Utah regulates mental-health chatbots. TherapOS is documentation software with mandatory therapist review, but features such as risk flags, mood inference and AI-suggested interventions need legal review per state before launch. Trackers disagree on 2026 additions, so verify against statutes.

Professional ethics and consent

Text-first design avoids audio recording and therefore APA Standard 4.03 recording consent. Therapists should still disclose AI use to clients; TherapOS will provide a disclosure template naming the tool and its vendors.

FDA

TherapOS is documentation software. It assists with note-writing — it does not make clinical decisions, does not produce diagnoses, and does not prescribe or recommend treatment. This places TherapOS outside FDA Software as a Medical Device (SaMD) Class II/III as long as we maintain this scope. Adding risk-scoring features that make specific clinical recommendations (e.g., "Patient has elevated suicide risk — recommend hospitalization") would require regulatory review. We have a policy commitment to consult a regulatory attorney before shipping any such feature.

India DPDP Act 2023 and Rules 2025

The DPDP Rules were notified on 13 November 2025. Consent-manager provisions apply from 13 November 2026 and most substantive duties from about 13 May 2027. Our plan, subject to legal confirmation:

  • Treat health data as high-risk regardless of statutory category. (To our reading the Act has no separate "sensitive personal data" class, although practitioner guides say otherwise; the Mental Healthcare Act 2017 also applies to consent.)
  • Host Indian accounts in an Indian region where feasible; assess cross-border transfer rules before sending any data to US AI vendors.
  • Explicit, granular consent and a Data Protection Officer or contact as the Rules require once applicable.
  • RCI (CRR) registration is mandatory only for clinical and rehabilitation psychologists; counsellors have no regulated title, so the India ICP needs defining.

§39 — AI Behavioral Contract

These rules are baked into every prompt template and cannot be overridden by user input:

  • Always a draft. Every AI output is explicitly labeled as a draft. Finalizing requires a deliberate therapist action.
  • Never fabricate. If information is missing, write "[Therapist to complete]" — never invent clinical details, quotes, or observations.
  • No diagnosis. No DSM codes, no clinical diagnoses in any AI outputs. Prompts explicitly prohibit this.
  • Editable at every step. Every AI-generated field is a plain text editor. The therapist can delete the entire draft.
  • Transparent. All AI content shows a ✦ AI indicator. Audit log records AI generation events separately from therapist edits.
  • Risk nudge — deferred. A soft "may warrant a safety review" prompt was previously planned for the MVP. It is now on hold pending legal review, because some state laws restrict AI mental-state detection and a false negative creates a false sense of safety. If shipped, it will be therapist-configurable, never a diagnosis, never a required action.
  • Modality-aware. Suggested questions and homework always respect the therapist's stated therapeutic modality.
