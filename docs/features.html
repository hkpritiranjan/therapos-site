Features

Every feature in TherapOS exists to answer one question: how can AI remove work instead of creating it? This page documents every feature in detail — usage, configuration, keyboard shortcuts, and what happens under the hood.

AI Session Notes

The core feature. Type your raw session notes — bullet points, shorthand, stream of consciousness. Click "Generate AI Draft" (⌘G). In 8–12 seconds, TherapOS produces a complete, professionally-worded clinical note using your therapeutic context.

Supported Note Formats

SOAP

Subjective — what the patient reported
Objective — therapist's behavioral observations
Assessment — clinical synthesis and progress
Plan — next session focus, homework, referrals

Most common

Assessment — clinical interpretation

Intervention — therapeutic techniques used

Free Text

Narrative prose note — no rigid sections. AI generates flowing clinical documentation. Good for certain specialties and supervisor-preferred formats.

Flexible

What Makes the AI Note Context-Aware

Unlike generic AI note generators, TherapOS injects your patient's full treatment arc into every note generation call. The AI sees:

  • Patient profile — name, primary concerns, diagnoses, therapeutic modality
  • Rolling AI summary — a 3–5 sentence summary updated after every finalized note, capturing the treatment arc, patterns, and current clinical picture
  • Last 5 session notes — truncated to 400 tokens each to stay within budget
  • Active treatment goals — what the therapist and patient are working toward
  • Recent homework — what was assigned and whether it was completed

On session 1, only your raw notes and patient profile are available. By session 5, the AI has a rich treatment arc. The output quality improves measurably with each session.

AI behavioral rules

The AI never diagnoses, never fabricates clinical details, and never auto-finalizes. Missing information gets "[Therapist to complete]" — not invented content. See AI & Prompts for the full rule set baked into every prompt.

Session Prep Briefs

Before every session (from session 2 onward), TherapOS generates a 90-second preparation brief that surfaces what actually matters. Open the session page — it's already there.

What the Brief Contains

Recap

2–3 sentences on what happened last session — key themes, breakthroughs, concerns the patient raised. Written for a therapist who hasn't looked at their notes since last week.

Suggested Questions

3–5 questions tailored to your therapeutic modality and current treatment arc. A CBT-modality patient gets different questions than a psychodynamic-modality patient.

Homework Check-In

How to approach checking in on assigned homework. Includes the specific homework title and a suggested follow-up framing.

Cross-Session Patterns

Notable patterns the AI has detected across recent sessions — e.g., "anxiety intensity correlates with upcoming family events." Null if no pattern is detected.

How It's Generated

The prep brief calls GPT-4o with enforced JSON response format. The system prompt instructs the model to use the patient's modality to shape suggested questions — CBT, DBT, psychodynamic, ACT, and EMDR each have distinct questioning styles. The brief is cached (Redis, 24h TTL) so it doesn't regenerate every time you open the session page.

Regenerating a Brief

If you've updated raw notes or the patient's goals since the brief was cached, click "Regenerate" in the prep panel. The brief will be refreshed and the cache updated.

Patient Memory (Rolling AI Summary)

After every finalized note, TherapOS automatically regenerates a 3–5 sentence AI summary of the patient's clinical arc. This runs as a BullMQ background job — you see the note as finalized immediately, and the summary updates within 30 seconds.

What the Summary Captures

  • What has improved since treatment began
  • What patterns or concerns persist
  • Recent breakthroughs or therapeutic milestones
  • Current clinical picture (how the patient is presenting now)
  • Key goals and progress

The summary is intentionally brief — 3–5 sentences max. It's not meant to replace the notes; it's meant to quickly orient the AI for the next call. You can view and edit the current summary in the patient profile under "AI Summary."

Editing the Summary

If the AI summary is inaccurate (it sometimes is, especially early in treatment), you can manually edit it in the patient profile. Your edit is preserved — the next refresh-summary job will incorporate it rather than overwriting it.

Natural Language Search

Press ⌘K anywhere in TherapOS to open the command palette. Natural language search runs across all finalized session notes via vector similarity search.

Example Queries

QueryWhat it finds
"What coping strategies have worked for Maya?"Notes mentioning successful coping interventions for this patient
"When did James first mention sleep problems?"Earliest session notes containing sleep-related content
"Homework David has skipped"Sessions where homework completion was noted as partial or not completed
"Sessions where we discussed his father"Notes containing references to the patient's father across all sessions
"Anxiety triggers for all my patients"Cross-patient search — surfaces anxiety trigger content across your practice

Scoping Your Search

By default, search is scoped to your entire practice. You can filter by patient using the patient dropdown in the search panel. Results show: the relevant snippet, session number, date, patient name (for cross-patient searches), and a relevance score.

How It Works

When you type a query, TherapOS embeds it using OpenAI's text-embedding-3-small (1,536 dimensions), then performs cosine similarity search against all NoteEmbedding vectors scoped to your therapist ID. Results are ordered by similarity score. The HNSW index makes this sub-100ms for up to ~500,000 embeddings.

ℹ
Search only covers finalized notes

Notes must be finalized and embedded before they're searchable. Embedding typically completes within 30 seconds of finalization. In-progress drafts and raw notes are not indexed.

Treatment Goals

Create and track treatment goals per patient. Goals are injected into every AI call — the AI knows what you're working toward and reflects that in notes and session prep.

Goal Lifecycle

StatusMeaningAI behavior
ActiveCurrently being worked onIncluded in every AI context call
AchievedGoal reached and documentedMentioned in summary as a win; removed from active context
PausedTemporarily deprioritizedNot included in active AI context
AbandonedNo longer relevantExcluded from AI context entirely

Goal Fields

  • Title: Short name — e.g., "Reduce workplace anxiety"
  • Description: Optional longer description — the AI uses this for context
  • Category: anxiety / depression / relationships / sleep / trauma / substance / career / self-esteem / other
  • Progress notes: Timestamped notes attached to any goal — linked to a specific session

Adding Progress Notes to a Goal

Open any goal on the patient profile and click "Add progress note." You can link the note to a specific session. Progress notes appear in the goal's history and are visible in the patient timeline. The AI doesn't automatically read progress note text, but goal status and recent progress are included in context.

Homework Generator

After a session, click "Suggest Homework" in the session panel. TherapOS calls GPT-4o with the session themes, active goals, and your therapeutic modality, and generates 3 homework options.

What Each Suggestion Includes

  • Title: Short, patient-friendly name for the assignment
  • Description: Detailed instructions written from the therapist's perspective — ready to share or adapt
  • Category: journaling / behavioral / exposure / mindfulness / social / psychoeducation
  • Rationale: Why this homework fits the session themes and modality — for your clinical judgment

Choose one, edit it, or dismiss all three and write your own. The selected or custom homework is saved to the patient's record and tracked for completion in the next session.

Tracking Completion

At the start of the next session, the session prep brief prompts you on how to approach the homework check-in. You can mark homework as Completed / Partial / Skipped from the session panel or the homework tab on the patient profile. Completion history is visible in the patient timeline.

Patient Timeline

Every patient has a chronological timeline showing all clinical activity in one scroll. Access it via the "Timeline" tab on the patient profile.

Timeline Events

Event typeWhat it shows

Click any session event to open the full session note. Click any goal event to jump to the goal on the patient profile. The timeline is read-only — editing happens in the session or goal context.

Patient Profile

The complete clinical record for a patient. Tabs: Overview · Sessions · Goals · Homework · Timeline · Settings.

Overview Tab

  • Patient info: name, DOB, contact, status
  • Clinical info: primary concerns, diagnoses, modality, session frequency
  • AI Summary: the rolling AI summary — viewable and editable
  • Active goals: quick view of open goals with status
  • Session stats: total sessions, last session date, average session frequency

Sessions Tab

Paginated list of all sessions — session number, date, type, status, note status (finalized / draft / not created). Click any row to open the session.

Goals Tab

All goals grouped by status (Active first). Each goal shows title, category, creation date, status, and a collapsible progress notes history.

Homework Tab

All homework items — filterable by status (Assigned / Completed / Partial / Skipped). Shows assigned session, due date, category, and completion notes.

Export and Data Portability

Your data is always exportable. From Settings → Export:

  • Patient PDF: Full clinical record for a single patient — all sessions, notes, goals, homework in a formatted PDF
  • Practice export: All patient data as JSON — includes all notes, goals, homework, audit log. For migration or archiving.

On cancellation, data is retained for 30 days in exportable form, then PHI is anonymized (audit logs are retained for 6 years per HIPAA requirements but PHI is removed from them).
