TherapOS: Competitor Research, Market Opportunity & Strategic Differentiation

TherapOS: Competitor Research, Market Opportunity & Strategic Differentiation. Researched 26 October 2026; a skeptical read of the market, with a continue/pivot/stop framework.

ℹ
How to read this page

Vendor pages were fetched that day; some fetches were truncated, blocked (403/404) or summarised, so every price should be re-checked on the live page before it goes in a deck. 1. Executive summary

QuestionAnswer
1. What problem?Independent therapists lose time to progress notes, re-reading old notes before sessions, and keeping goals and homework coherent across sessions. TherapOS drafts notes from the therapist's own typed or dictated text, keeps an editable client summary, and produces pre-session briefs. How much time this saves is unmeasured. Unknown
2. Who already solves it?In the US, almost everyone credible: Upheal, Mentalyc, Blueprint, Berries, Twofold, Supanote, Carepatron, Freed, Heidi, plus EHR-bundled AI from SimplePractice and TherapyNotes. In India, no product found combines therapy-specific AI notes with patient memory; the closest are a cheap admin tool (ProTherapy) and doctor scribes (EkaScribe, RxNote). Verified/Inferred
3. Direct overlapBlueprint (notes, session prep, treatment plans, worksheets; works beside an EHR; $0.99/session), Upheal (free text-only tier, Golden Thread treatment plans, paid session-prep assistant), Mentalyc (notes build on past notes, treatment plans; $29.99/month on annual Basic), Berries and Twofold (pre-session summaries, any-EHR). Verified
4. Adjacent threatsSimplePractice Care Aide (announced 17 Jun 2026: session prep, treatment planner beta, client summaries with homework) and TherapyNotes Session Assist bundle prep into the EHR clinicians already pay for. Nextvisit has a psychiatry patient timeline. Limbic and Wysa-style patient chatbots are substitutes for between-session support but sit on the other side of Illinois/Nevada rules. Verified/Reported
5. Differentiated today?Barely. Text-first/no-audio is real but narrow: Upheal's free tier also accepts text, and Mentalyc, Twofold and Blueprint accept typed or dictated input. What is still thin in public marketing is a strict no-recording, no-transcript-retention posture, and deep, inspectable cross-session memory. Inferred
6. Hypotheses onlyThat therapists will pay for memory/prep as a standalone add-on; that source-linked summaries build trust; that clinician-approved activities with patient reflections that feed the next prep brief are wanted and safe; that Indian clinicians will pay; that text-first matters to buyers. None is validated.
7. Worth pursuing?Only as a narrow, validated bet. Note drafting is commoditised (Carepatron and Upheal have free tiers; Berries has a free tier) and the $29 price is level with Mentalyc's annual plan. The serviceable market supports a small business (~$0.35M–1.6M ARR in our scenarios, §12), not a venture outcome, unless the activity loop proves out or clinic accounts raise ARPU.
8. Build next3 mo: harden the documentation-first MVP, run discovery (§13). 6 mo: source-linked summaries, goal tracking, copy-to-EHR export. 12 mo: a small activity-and-reflection pilot, only if gates pass. 24 mo: clinic roles; India only on pull. Detail in §15.
⚠
Preliminary recommendation: proceed with a narrower scope, conditionally

Do not keep selling 'AI notes for therapists' as the story; it is a crowded commodity. Proceed for 30 days as a validation sprint on one segment (independent US therapists who already use AI and keep a separate EHR) and one question: does source-linked patient memory plus clinician-approved between-session activities earn repeat use and payment? If the answer is no on the §14 gates, defer. India is deferred, not cancelled.

What the US scan means

  • No longer differentiators: AI notes themselves; "audio deleted after transcription"; BAA at signup; SOC 2; "never trains on your data"; "works with any EHR"; treatment plans; pre-session summaries; homework recaps.
  • Why a clinician would pick a competitor over TherapOS: it is free (Carepatron, Upheal, Berries free tier); it is bundled in their EHR (SimplePractice, TherapyNotes); it has live ambient capture (most); it has an established security story (SOC 2 reports) that TherapOS does not yet have.
  • Where TherapOS can compete: strict text-first with no transcript retention (competitors vary: SimplePractice default opt-in, Twofold opt-in retention, Mentalyc keeps anonymised transcripts); deep, inspectable memory; flat pricing for high-caseload users against per-session models.

How practitioners work today

ProTherapy's own listing says solo therapists "juggle Calendly, WhatsApp, Google Forms and spreadsheets" (Reported, vendor marketing). We found no survey of WhatsApp, spreadsheet, paper or ChatGPT use among Indian therapists (Unknown). Those workarounds are substitutes; they are not evidence of unmet, payable demand.

Workforce (conflicting, so treat as ranges)

  • RCI-registered clinical psychologists: 1,181 (Rajya Sabha reply, July 2023 Reported) versus 3,890 registered with ~2,900 active in a later study Reported. The gap is unexplained; check the CRR directly.
  • Psychiatrists: ~9,000 (2019 IJP supplement Reported); IPS directory ~8,600 members; an NHRC figure of 2,052. No NMC count found Unknown.
  • Counsellors: unregulated, no count Unknown.

Regulation that touches an AI notes tool

  • DPDP Rules 2025 (published 13 Nov 2025): consent-manager rule 13 Nov 2026; notice, consent, security safeguards, breach intimation and data-principal rights from 13 May 2027 Verified PIB and Reported law-firm summaries. Constitutional challenges reported pending Reported. Cross-border transfer and health-data treatment need a lawyer.
  • Mental Healthcare Act 2017 s.23 confidentiality, s.24 restrictions on release, s.25 client access to basic records. No AI-specific source found; vendors and sub-processors likely need confidentiality coverage Inferred.

What to remove from the pitch today

  • "Only product with memory / prep" (false: Upheal, Blueprint, Berries, SimplePractice, TherapyNotes all have versions).
  • Any time-saving number, "HIPAA-ready", "signed BAAs", or SOC 2 language (none exist yet).
  • "Cheapest" (Carepatron, Upheal free tiers; Mentalyc annual).
  • Any implication that AI decides treatment or monitors risk.

Recommended positioning

Statement

TherapOS is the private memory layer for your caseload: notes you can trust, context you can inspect, and activities you approve, without recording a single session and without replacing your EHR.

Tagline

"Remember every client. Record nothing." (working; test against "Your caseload, remembered.")

Why adopt alongside the current system: the EHR stays the legal record; TherapOS does the thinking work around it, drafting from your own words and showing where every summary line came from. If it cannot be trusted line by line, it has no reason to exist, which is why provenance is the first build, not the last.

10. Patient-specific activities and homework

Ten-step flow

  1. Clinician selects a goal already recorded on the client.
  2. Chooses an approach they are trained in (CBT, ACT, DBT or other).
  3. Picks an exercise from a reviewed library or asks for a draft.
  4. Personalises language, duration, frequency, difficulty, format.
  5. Reviews and approves; nothing is shared before this.
  6. Shares via a secure expiring link (no app install).
  7. Patient completes, skips, or taps "I need help" (routes to the clinician, with a plain notice that it is not monitored in real time).
  8. Patient adds a reflection (optional).
  9. Clinician reviews responses in the next prep brief, labelled patient-reported.
  10. Clinician adjusts the activity or goal.

Safeguards (non-negotiable design rules)

  • AI output is a draft until a clinician approves it; approval and revisions are logged.
  • Activities may use only documented goals and clinician-entered preferences; no invented history; automated check rejects facts not in the source.
  • The clinician selects and adapts; patient participation is optional and accessible (plain language, readable fonts, no streaks or guilt mechanics).
  • Patient responses are visible only to the authorised clinician; stored as PHI under a BAA-covered path.
  • Completion is shown as engagement, never as proof of improvement.
  • No crisis monitoring, no real-time response promise unless a staffed, validated service exists; the patient screen states this and shows local emergency options.
  • Patient data not sent to third parties without legal basis; names pseudonymised before model calls.
  • AI never converses with the patient in therapeutic mode (Illinois HB 1806 and Nevada AB 406 risk; both Reported).

Versus existing tools and the evidence

Quenza and Therapist Aid provide libraries; Blueprint sends worksheets and AI-assisted reminders; SimplePractice client summaries can include homework; Mentalyc has a worksheet library. We found no public description of an activity drafted from this client's documented plan, approved by the clinician, with patient reflections flowing back into the next prep brief (Inferred, marketing pages only; Blueprint is the nearest). Evidence for homework is correlational: Kazantzis 2016 (17 studies, N=2,312) finds g≈0.79 association between CBT homework compliance and outcomes, but assigning homework is not shown to cause it, and self-reported compliance gives weaker effects. Routine outcome monitoring effects are small (De Jong 2021: d≈0.15, Reported). So the loop cannot honestly claim better outcomes; its claim is better continuity, to be measured.

Could it differentiate more than documentation? Probably, because it is harder to copy and creates daily patient-side usage that raises retention. But it also adds PHI surface, regulatory exposure and a second user type. Verdict: a gated pilot, not an MVP feature.

21. Founder decision memo

Should we proceed? Yes, narrowly and conditionally: a 30-day validation sprint, not a scale-up. AI notes alone are commoditised.

First customer: a US independent therapist (LCSW/LMFT/LPC or counselling psychologist) who already uses AI or ChatGPT and keeps an EHR.

Strongest differentiated opportunity: inspectable, source-linked client memory, with a clinician-approved activity loop as the gated upside.

Smallest product to validate: today's app plus source links on summaries, copy-to-EHR export and pseudonymised AI calls. No patient surface yet; test the activity loop first as a clickable prototype.

Evidence needed in 30 days: 15 therapist interviews with observed (not stated) pain; 5 trials of rivals; ≥5 of 15 offering money at ≥$25; prototype comprehension ≥70%; patient interviews via clinicians.

Three biggest failure reasons: (1) free or bundled AI is good enough, so no one pays a standalone fee; (2) therapists will not trust a new vendor with client data without a BAA/SOC 2, and the compliance cost outweighs a ≈$0.3–1.6M ARR ceiling; (3) the activity loop proves unwanted or too risky, leaving only commodity notes.

Milestones to justify 6–12 more months: ≥6 of 10 pilot clinicians weekly-active unprompted; ≥50% source-link opens; paid conversions at ≥$25; a clean counsel opinion on patient reflections; BAA path secured.

Pitch deck today claims: a working text-first drafting and memory product; a researched, honest competitive map; a 30-day validation plan. Must not claim: time saved, HIPAA/SOC 2/BAA status, "only product with memory", clinical outcomes, traction, India demand, or the $1M–$5M MRR targets.
