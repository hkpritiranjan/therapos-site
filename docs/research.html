Market & Regulatory Research

Evidence base for the TherapOS plan: market size, AI adoption, competitors, HIPAA, state AI laws and India DPDP. Each claim is tagged by confidence. Compiled 25 October 2026.

ℹ
How to read this page

Research compiled 25 October 2026 from public web sources. Each claim carries a confidence tag: Confirmed official or primary source; Reported secondary source, vendor blog or compliance tracker — directionally useful, verify before relying on it; Unverified an assumption of ours with no source. Most competitor material is written by competitors. This is product research, not legal advice.

1. Summary: what the research changed

Still true

AI use among therapists is rising fast, the buyer is price-sensitive, and the "keep your EHR, add TherapOS" positioning avoids a painful migration.

No longer true

"No competitor has session prep and cross-session memory." Upheal, Mentalyc and SimplePractice all ship cross-session features. The moat claim has to be rebuilt on something else.

Not supportable yet

The "35% of time / 16 minutes per session / 77% fatigue" statistics, the "signed BAAs with Anthropic and OpenAI" claim, and "HIPAA-ready" marketing. See §8.

New opportunity

Text-first input (no audio recording) sidesteps recording-consent rules, transcript retention and the psychotherapy-notes grey area that ambient scribes must manage.

What this means for the plan

A $29 seat is a small-business outcome unless it expands into clinics (higher ARPU) or adjacent revenue. The 2029–2030 targets of ~$1M and $3–5M MRR would need roughly 30,000–150,000 paying seats at $29–$49, which is a large share of the entire serviceable population. Treat those years as aspirational and plan the next 18 months against the 1–5% rows. See the reconciliation in Plan §40.

3. Demand: are therapists adopting AI?

  • Adoption is rising quickly. APA's 2025 Practitioner Pulse Survey: psychologists who never used AI in practice fell from 71% to 44% in a year. Confirmed — APA Monitor, Behavioral Health Business.
  • Regular use is still minority. 8% daily, 14% weekly, ~29% at least monthly. Reported
  • Use is administrative. Top uses: writing assistance, content generation, summarisation. Only 8% and 5% use AI for clinical diagnosis and client assistance. Reported This matches TherapOS's documentation-only scope.
  • Trust is the barrier. ~9 in 10 psychologists report concerns about AI; about two-thirds worry about data breaches. Reported
  • Other professions agree. AAMFT's 2025 workforce study: 52% of MFTs use AI for administrative tasks such as note-taking. Reported — AAMFT. Different surveys use different windows, so do not compare the figures directly.

Implication: demand is not the constraint; credibility is. A product that is transparent about data flow, always shows its sources, and never overclaims on compliance has a real edge over louder competitors.

4. The documentation-burden claim

Our earlier copy said therapists lose 35% of working time to documentation, spend 16 minutes per session on notes, and that 77% report mental fatigue. A focused search found no reliable primary survey measuring psychotherapist documentation time. The nearest figures were:

  • A vendor page claiming 15–20 hours/week, attributed to unlinked "NASW and APA workforce surveys." Unverified
  • The APTA's 2025 survey of ~19,000 physical therapists (91% say administrative burden contributes to burnout). A different profession and mostly about prior authorisation. Reported

Action taken: the unsourced statistics have been removed from the site. The ROI page now uses a transparent, adjustable assumption, and we will replace it with measured numbers from beta users (time-to-final-note, edit rate). See Case Study — Measuring.

6. Regulation and compliance

HIPAA: psychotherapy notes versus progress notes

Under 45 CFR 164.501, psychotherapy notes are a mental-health professional's notes documenting or analysing the content of a counselling session, kept separate from the rest of the record. They get extra protection and generally need specific authorisation to disclose. The definition excludes medication monitoring, session times, modalities and frequencies, test results, and summaries of diagnosis, functional status, treatment plan, symptoms, prognosis and progress. Confirmed for the definition; Reported for how it applies to AI output — we found no authoritative guidance.

✦
Product implication

TherapOS finalized notes are progress notes (part of the record). Raw session notes the therapist types are closer to process / psychotherapy notes. Design rule: raw notes are treated as the most sensitive data, stay out of analytics and embeddings by default, and the therapist can choose to keep them off the AI path entirely. This is a Phase-1 requirement, not an afterthought. Get counsel to confirm the classification.

AI vendors and BAAs

  • OpenAI: signs BAAs for API customers, but only for endpoints that qualify for zero data retention (ZDR); without ZDR, API traffic can be retained up to 30 days. Approval is case-by-case. Reported — OpenAI HIPAA guide.
  • Anthropic: offers HIPAA-ready API access with a BAA for eligible customers, conditioned on specific eligible services (such as zero-retention). Excludes Workbench/Console, consumer plans and beta features. Reported — Anthropic docs.
  • Implication: "signed BAAs with Anthropic and OpenAI" is a to-do, not a fact. Until executed, no real patient data may touch either API, and the product must be marketed as pre-launch. The current app also sends patient names to OpenAI in prompts; this must change (pseudonymise before the model call).

Professional ethics

APA guidance expects informed consent that explains the purpose, risks and data flow of AI tools. Standard 4.03 requires permission before recording a client's voice. Reported — APA AI ethical guidance. We found no NASW-specific AI guidance; verify directly. TherapOS should ship a client-disclosure template that therapists can use, naming the tool and the vendors.

India

  • The DPDP Rules were notified on 13 November 2025; consent-manager provisions (Rule 4) apply from 13 Nov 2026, and most substantive duties from about 13 May 2027. Reported — PIB.
  • Correction: our earlier docs said health data is "Sensitive Personal Data under the DPDP Act 2023." As far as we can tell the Act has no separate sensitive-data category, although practitioner guides claim otherwise. Check the Act text; treat health data as high-risk regardless. The Mental Healthcare Act 2017 also applies to consent.
  • RCI (CRR) registration is legally required for clinical and rehabilitation psychologists. "Counsellor" is not a registered title, so the India ICP is fuzzy. Reported — TherapyRoute.

FDA

Our position, unchanged: documentation assistance, no diagnosis, no treatment recommendations. Note that "suggested interventions" in the prep brief and the homework generator sit closer to clinical decision support than pure documentation. Keep them clearly framed as drafts for the therapist and have a regulatory attorney review before scaling. Unverified
